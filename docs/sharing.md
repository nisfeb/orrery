# Sharing a body with another ship

A body and its observations can be shared with another ship: read mode mirrors what you know onto their ship, edit mode also carries what they observe back onto yours. The unit is one body. Nothing else on the ship is visible to them.

## How it works

- `POST /apps/orrery/api/share` with `{"id": "person/sarah", "ship": "~sampel-palnet", "mode": "read"}` (or `"edit"`) records the share in `shares.json`, lets `~sampel-palnet` read the body's directory (a usergroup named `orrery-person.sarah` with one peek grant), and pokes an offer into `~sampel-palnet`'s inbox. The answer's `notified` is false only when the other ship vetoed the poke or nacked it. A poke that timed out answers true: grubbery's remote acks are unobservable and a poke that times out usually landed. The first offer assumes the other ship installed orrery at the standard desk path; a ship that did not answers `notified` false.
- On `~sampel-palnet`, `GET /apps/orrery/api/shares` lists `offers`, `accepted` and `shares` (what this ship shares out). `POST /api/accept` with `{"host": "~your-ship", "id": "person/sarah"}` takes an offer. A local body that already carries the offered ship is where it lands, whatever either side calls it. Failing that, a body whose ship is `~sampel-palnet` itself lands on `person/me`, the host's own `person/me` lands on `person/your-ship` (its name without the sig), and any other body lands under its own id. The body is created with the offered name and ship only when it is not there yet. `POST /api/decline` drops an offer.
- The follower runs every five minutes, on every accept and on `POST /api/sync`. It reads each accepted body whole from the host and submits the host's own observations to the local writer with `by` set to the host and `source` `{"kind": "ship", "id": "~your-ship/<host observation id>"}`. A row the host retracts is retracted here.
- What crosses the wire is the whole directory. The peer ship reads every row the host holds on that body, with its source pointer, its actor, its note, and the rows the host mirrored from third ships. What orrery stores on the peer is narrower: only the host's own rows, and their source pointer is replaced by the host's grub name. A third ship's rows are not stored, and the peer's owner can still read them with a plain remote peek.
- In edit mode the follower also sends the local observations on that body (the ones not mirrored from a ship) to the host's inbox, where they land with `by` set to the sender and the same source shape. Retractions travel the same way. The host's inbox checks the share record before it applies anything; the sender is the transport's, never the payload's.
- `DELETE /apps/orrery/api/share/person/sarah/~sampel-palnet` removes the ship from the record and the group and tells the other ship, which drops the accepted row. Mirrored observations stay on both sides, still naming their source.

## Sharing a sphere (version 90)

A sphere is shared whole: every body filed under it (for `sphere/home`, every body filed under nothing as well), every row on them, both ways in edit mode, each row keeping its author.

- `POST /api/sphere-share` with `{"sphere": "sphere/home", "ship": "~sampel-palnet", "mode": "edit"}` records it in `sphere-shares.json` (apart from the body shares in `shares.json`, since a sphere is also a body that can be shared alone), lets that ship read `/feeds/<slug>/` (a usergroup `orrery-feed.<slug>` with one peek grant on that directory and nothing else), and pokes an offer into its inbox. `POST /api/sphere-accept` with `{"host", "sphere"}` takes it on the other ship, which lands it on its own sphere body (its twin, else the same id) and writes the twin. `DELETE /api/sphere-share/<kind>/<slug>/<ship>` ends it; the other ship stops reading and closes its own feed to us. What each holds stays. `GET /api/shares` lists `sphere_shares`, `sphere_offers` and `sphere_follows`.
- The feed: a fiber that keeps the change beacon writes, once a burst of writes has been still for twenty seconds (two minutes at most), each shared sphere's new rows and the owner's new corrections on its bodies as entries `{"seq", "op": "row" or "correct", "name", "as", "row"}` into pages of a hundred (`/feeds/<slug>/p<n>.json`) and moves `/feeds/<slug>/head.json`, then pokes each reading ship `{"action": "feed-moved"}`. The writer is untouched. What went in is kept in `feed-state.json`, row by row with the retracted flag it went with, so a retraction goes in when it happens and a row is never sent twice.
- Never in the feed: a row from another ship (so nothing echoes), a `twin`, an attribute in `policy.sensitive`, a row the owner keeps private (`POST /api/private {"id": <row id>, "private": true}`), a `sphere` row naming any sphere but this one (so no other sphere is told it exists), and a correction a ship made.
- The reader runs in the follower, every five minutes and on each wake: it reads the head, then a page at a time from where it left off, its place kept after each page. A page that makes bodies waits until the writer has made them (twenty seconds at most) before their rows go in; failing that, the page is read again on the next pass rather than its rows dropped. Each entry lands on the body its `as` names for this ship (the sender's twin for it), else on its id read as a ref (twin, the sender's self as the body carrying its ship, else as written); a body new here is made under the sender's name for it with its twin. An entry about a body this ship holds that is not in the sphere here is refused, unless it files the body there: a body filed out on one side takes nothing more from the other. Rows land as the sender's, refs read this ship's way; a correction is made here as the sender's. A `sphere` row carried in is the other owner's filing and is kept at once, not held for the owner to confirm as a model's would be.
- Pairing (version 91): before a reader takes any row, it matches the sender's members to its own bodies. The feed writer keeps a roster beside the pages (`/feeds/<slug>/roster.json`: each member's id, kind, name, ship, aliases, calendar uids and twins).
  - **Sure matches** have their twins written at once, and the reader waits until they are written. A match is sure by the sender's twin for it here, by the same ship (the sender's body for this ship's owner is `person/me`), or by the same calendar event. Two bodies there carrying one ship may both pair with one body here.
  - **Matches by kind and name** wait on the follow row's `pending` until the owner says (`GET /api/pairing`, `POST /api/pairing {"key", "there", "same"}`, the To pair card on the Spheres page). "Same" writes the twin; "different" lets the sender's body come as one of its own. A name that matches two bodies here matches none.
  - **Holding:** the reader reads no page while a match waits.
  - **Joining:** a body a page files under the sphere counts as in it for that whole page.
- Shared actions (version 92): an action about the sphere's bodies only (and about something) goes in the feed as an `act` entry each time its status moves.
  - **Assignee.** A task's payload may name who does it, `"assignee": {"ref": "person/x"}`; the schema's task shape says so to the models.
  - **Not carried out here.** An approved action assigned to someone other than `person/me` is not run by the executor.
  - **Crossing.** On the reading ship, an action assigned to its owner (the ref read its way) and approved where it was proposed is filed as a proposal from that ship, with `payload.twin` naming the original. It waits for the owner whatever the policy says.
  - **Status both ways.** When either copy ends (done, failed, dismissed), the other is moved to the same end, by the ship that ended it, where the move is legal; a copy already ended is left alone, so nothing echoes.
  - **The other ship's open actions** are kept in `peer-actions.json`. The writer will not file one of the same kind and title, unless it is that action crossing. The generator is shown them as their own part, untagged, as the other owner's.
- Edit mode is two feeds, not a push: the ship that accepts keeps a feed of the sphere for the host and tells it where (`{"action": "sphere-accept"}`), and the host reads it only while its own record still shares the sphere with that ship in edit mode. The design's `share-rows` inbox operation is not used; the inbox carries only the offer, the accept, the revoke and the wake.
- The two-ship check is `scripts/sphere-matrix.py`, on a test sphere: never `sphere/home`, which is every unfiled body.

## Location for a while (version 88)

A position is not a body, so it travels apart from body shares. `POST /api/location/share` with `{"ship": "~sampel-palnet", "hours": 2}` (or `"home": true`, or both, and `"precision": "exact"` for the phone's own figures) keeps a grant in `location.json`. The location loop runs on each fix the phone sends and every ten minutes. It pokes each live grant's ship once per new fix, `{"action": "position", "lat", "lon", "acc", "at", "until", "home"}` into the same inbox road as offers, and `{"action": "position-end"}` when the grant's time passes, when the owner reaches home on an until-home share after being seen away, or on `DELETE /api/location/share/<ship>`. The receiving inbox keeps the latest fix per ship in its own `location.json`, only when a person there carries the sender's ship (any other is refused and noted in `/tr/inbox`), and drops it on the end or when its `until` passes. Neither ship files a position as a fact, and nothing is read: no grant, no group.

## What to know

- The ask grows by poke on `/sys/gall/`, `/sys/behn/` and `/sys/ames/registry`, peek on `/sys/ames/usergroups/` and `/sys/ames/ships/`, make on `/sys/ames/usergroups/`. Refuse them and everything else keeps working; sharing is off.
- Since version 89 a `{"ref"}` value is read the receiving ship's way as it lands, in both directions (rows mirrored from the host, rows pushed back in edit mode). A body's twin here comes first: the `twin` fact `{"ship", "id"}` says a body here is that ship's body under that id. The accept writes one on the body a share lands on, so sharing the person you keep for the other ship's owner lands on their `person/me` and from then on your rows naming that person mean them there. Failing a twin, the sender's `person/me` is the body here carrying the sender's ship (else `person/<sender>`, where an accept lands the sender's self), and `person/<this ship>` is `person/me`. Anything else arrives as written. A row read before its twin was known is retracted and read again when the twin arrives. A twin fact never crosses either way. One that still names the receiving body itself is refused and noted once in the ship-traffic log (`/tr/inbox`).
- An offer names the calendar uids the shared body's rows carry (twenty at most). A ship that reads the same calendar accepts it onto its own body for that event, whatever either side calls it, before falling back to the id the host uses.
- The name and ship of a shared body are copied when the accept creates it here; a body that already exists keeps its own name. Aliases are not carried; observations are.
- Every accepted row carries `last` (the last attempt, successful or not) and `error` (empty, or why the host could not be read or would not take the edits). Deleting the local body the share lands on stops the pass with `the shared body does not exist here any more`. In edit mode the host's acknowledgement means it received the rows, not that it kept them: rows sent after the host narrowed the share are dropped there and noted in its ship-traffic log (`/tr/inbox`). A push that timed out counts as pushed, since the host answered the peek a moment earlier.
- A share can be narrowed by the host at any time and it applies at once, and a wider offer still waiting in the inbox goes with it, so it cannot be accepted afterwards. Widening one (read to edit) is a new offer: nothing of yours leaves the ship until you accept it.
- A ship that is down does not stall the follower: a peek or a poke gives up after thirty seconds and the row records it.
- Offers, revokes and edits from other ships are logged in `/tr/inbox`, a ring of 500 of their own, so nothing a stranger sends can push the owner's audit log out of `/tr/log`. It is read through the ball browser at `GET /grubbery/ball/apps/shell.shell/desks/orrery.desk/desk/data/orrery.orrery_app/tr/inbox?raw=1`.
- Retention applies to mirrored rows too: a row older than `retention_days` by the later of when it was true and when the ship recorded it can be culled here and mirrored again from the host, and the map of what was pushed grows for the life of a share.
- A carried row's id is a hash over the fields orrery stores, its source pointer among them, so the same claim read twice makes one grub here.
- A share carries the body whole: every attribute on it, the sensitive ones included, with its notes, its actors and its source pointers. The read grant is a grant on a directory and cannot filter by attribute, so a body holding facts you would not share is not a body to share.
- The push is the one direction that can be filtered, and it is: in edit mode an attribute named in `policy.sensitive` is never sent to the host, live or retracted.
- Deleting a shared body drops its share record and empties its group, so the ships it was shared with lose the peek with the body.
